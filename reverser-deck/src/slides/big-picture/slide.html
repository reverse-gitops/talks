REVERSER · THE BIG PICTURE
Settings are data. Give them their own home.
Orders stay in the app. Settings move behind a data-only API that users never notice, and from there into Git.
01 Today: the app keeps its settings in its own database, right next to the orders.
02 Move them out: KCP serves part of the customer-facing API, as a model of connected kinds. Same domain, same login.
03 Alice's settings page is just a client of that API: it reads with plain GETs, using her own login.
04 Reverser turns settings changes into commits. Git and the OCI registry close the loop back to the app.
05 Alice makes the same typo. It is committed, deployed, and emails fail again. But now you know who, when, and what.
06 Bob opens the config repo. Every change is a commit with a name on it, so he sees who changed the key and when.
06 Bob reverts in Git. Flux syncs KCP, the fix ships to the app, and the emails flow again.
